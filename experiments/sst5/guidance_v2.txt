These are snippets (sentences or phrases) from movie reviews, rated in the Stanford Sentiment Treebank style: judge the sentiment the snippet itself expresses about the film, as a typical reader would feel it on first reading.
- 4 (very positive) is NOT rare: use it whenever the snippet gives clear, enthusiastic praise without reservations, even if short. Strong praise words (wonderful, brilliant, gripping, very funny, beautiful, best, marvelous, inspiring, heartwarming, fiercely clever, robust entertainment, a must-see / no reason to miss) -> 4. Use 3 for mild, moderate or qualified praise ("solid", "manages to", "some good moments", "pleasant", "smart mix", "wittier version of") and for positive-leaning descriptions of the film's subject.
- Descriptions of the film that use vivid, approving or evocative language (builds to a crescendo, a testament of, combines X with a fantasy of redemption) lean 3, not 2.
- 0 (very negative) is for scathing, emphatic condemnation (worst, disaster, unwatchable, insulting, "one of the biggest disappointments", mockery or contempt, e.g. a dismissive put-down like "a bland yawner" or a sneering comparison). Ordinary criticism (dull, lazy, banal, overburdened, routine, formulaic, uneven) is usually 1, even when phrased sharply.
- 2 (neutral) is ONLY for snippets with no evaluative stance (plot descriptions, factual statements, fragments, rhetorical setups). Lukewarm or mixed verdicts are NOT neutral in this dataset: "a mixed bag", "a matter of taste", "if you're into that, have at it", "strong performances, but neither reaches ...", "an enthralling premise hindered by uneven dialogue" -> 1 (negative), because the overall verdict is a reservation. Mixed snippets whose final weight is clearly approving ("never recovers from its premise, but sustains an enjoyable level of ridiculousness") -> 2 or 3.
- Sarcasm and backhanded compliments count as negative.
When giving probabilities, spread probability over the adjacent levels you are unsure between.
